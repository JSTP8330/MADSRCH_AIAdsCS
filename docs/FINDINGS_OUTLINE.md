# Overall Findings: AI-Labeled Ads (DLSU Students)

**Who:** 92 male and 92 female DLSU undergraduates  
**What we measured:** How students react when ads are clearly labeled as AI-made  
**How:** Survey percentages (including % who agreed, ratings 4–5)

---

## Key Findings

### Trust and credibility
- Few students say AI-labeled ads are more creative (about **8%** of women, **11%** of men).
- Many say these ads feel **less authentic** (about **67%** women, **69%** men).
- Many also say AI-labeled ads are more likely to be **misleading** (about **57%** women, **63%** men).
- When they see an AI label, **67%** of women and **52%** of men view the brand more **negatively**. Almost no one views it more positively.

### Engagement
- Few students are likely to stop, click, like, or share AI-labeled ads.
- Most are likely to **ignore** them (**62%** women, **76%** men).
- When choosing between a human-made and AI-made ad for the same product, most prefer **human-made** (**81%** women, **75%** men).

### Purchase
- Learning that an ad used AI makes many students **less likely to buy**.
- This is stronger among women (**62%** less likely) than men (**45%** less likely).
- Only a small share say AI makes them more likely to buy.

### Awareness
- In this valid sample, most already noticed AI-labeled ads; more women said “Yes,” while more men said “Not sure.”

---

## What this means

- An AI label can hurt brand trust, even when students are used to seeing AI ads.
- Students still prefer human-made ads for attention and engagement.
- Women show a stronger negative brand reaction and a stronger drop in purchase interest.
- Low click/share rates do not mean students accept AI ads; many simply ignore them.

---

## Recommendations

**For brands and marketers**
- Pair AI disclosure with clear proof the product and brand are real (reviews, real people, brand accountability).
- Prefer “AI-assisted / human-directed” framing over a pure “Made with AI” look when targeting students.
- Check trust and purchase intent separately from likes and clicks.
- Review results by gender; a one-size creative plan may underperform with female students.

**For schools / digital literacy**
- Teach students how to read AI labels critically without rejecting every AI ad by default.
- Support transparent, honest disclosure standards for campus-facing campaigns.
