# Overall Findings: AI-Labeled Ads (DLSU Students, Both Genders Combined)
**Who:** 184 DLSU undergraduates (92 male + 92 female, pooled)  
**What we measured:** How students react when ads are clearly labeled as AI-made  
**How:** Survey percentages for the full sample (including % who agreed, ratings 4–5)

Gender differences are in the separate Male vs Female report (`FINDINGS_OUTLINE.md` / `findings_summary.xlsx`).

---

## Key Findings
### Trust and credibility
- Few students say AI-labeled ads are more creative (about **9%**).
- Many say these ads feel **less authentic** (about **68%**).
- Many also say AI-labeled ads are more likely to be **misleading** (about **60%**).
- When they see an AI label, about **60%** view the brand more **negatively**. Almost no one views it more positively (about **5%**).

### Engagement
- Few students are likely to stop, click, like, or share AI-labeled ads.
- Most are likely to **ignore** them (about **69%**).
- When choosing between a human-made and AI-made ad for the same product, most prefer **human-made** (about **78%**).

### Purchase
- Learning that an ad used AI makes many students **less likely to buy** (about **53%** much or somewhat less likely).
- About **40%** say it has no effect on their decision.
- Only a small share say AI makes them more likely to buy (about **7%**).

### Awareness
- In this valid sample, most already noticed AI-labeled ads (**89%** Yes; **11%** Not sure).

---

## What this means

- An AI label can hurt brand trust, even when students are used to seeing AI ads.
- Students still prefer human-made ads for attention and engagement.
- Low click/share rates do not mean students accept AI ads; many simply ignore them.
- Pooled results describe the sample as a whole; check the gender-split report for where men and women diverge (brand reaction, purchase).

---

## Recommendations

**For brands and marketers**
- Pair AI disclosure with clear proof the product and brand are real (reviews, real people, brand accountability).
- Prefer “AI-assisted / human-directed” framing over a pure “Made with AI” look when targeting students.
- Check trust and purchase intent separately from likes and clicks.
- Review results by gender as well; a one-size creative plan may underperform with some groups.

**For schools / digital literacy**
- Teach students how to read AI labels critically without rejecting every AI ad by default.
- Support transparent, honest disclosure standards for campus-facing campaigns.
